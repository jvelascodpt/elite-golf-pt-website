Golf Back Pain Physical Therapy

Lower back pain is the most common injury in golf. We find out why your back is doing too much of the work, and fix that, so you can play without it.

Book Your Evaluation

Why golf causes it

Your lower back is built for stability, not rotation. When your hips or mid-back don’t turn well, the golf swing still needs that rotation from somewhere, and the lower back ends up supplying it, hundreds of times a round.

That is why stretching the back on its own rarely holds. The back is where it hurts, but it is usually not where the problem starts.

Stiff hips

Limited hip rotation, especially in the lead hip, forces the spine to turn instead.

A stiff mid-back

If the thoracic spine can’t rotate, the lower back takes over its job.

The “crunch” at impact

Side-bending and extension at speed load the small joints of the lower spine.

Core and hip control

If you can’t stabilize at speed, the spine absorbs force it shouldn’t.

How we treat it

  1. 01

    Evaluation. A TPI Medical Level 3 screen, mobility and strength testing, and a look at your swing, to find what is driving the pain.

  2. 02

    Hands-on treatment. Soft tissue work and joint mobilization to calm pain and restore motion.

  3. 03

    Golf-specific exercise. A home and range program for the mobility, strength and control your swing needs.

  4. 04

    Back to full golf. A staged return, from short game to full swings, so the pain doesn’t come back.

See a physician first if

  • Numbness in the groin or inner thighs, or new trouble controlling your bladder or bowels: go to an emergency room.
  • Leg weakness that is getting worse, or numbness spreading down the leg.
  • Back pain after a fall or accident, or with fever or unexplained weight loss.

Common questions

Should I stop playing golf with back pain?

Not always. Many golfers can keep playing with changes to practice volume, warm-up and swing while they rehab. Your evaluation tells you what is safe to keep doing.

Can physical therapy help a herniated or bulging disc?

Many disc-related back problems improve with physical therapy. We assess how your back responds to movement and load, and refer you to a physician when imaging or another opinion is needed.

Do I need an MRI first?

Usually not. Most back pain in golfers doesn’t need imaging to start treatment. If you already have imaging, bring it to your visit.
